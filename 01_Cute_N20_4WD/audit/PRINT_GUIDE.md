# Prototype printing and fit guide

**Use only `../exports/*.stl`.** Every mesh is in millimetres, starts at Z=0, and has validated closed topology. Camera, ToF and wheel exports already use the preferred print rotation. The complete assembly STL and meshes under `audit/components`, `baseline_meshes` or `hardware_checks` are inspection artifacts.

**Production readiness remains unverified.** Exact motors, boards, battery, hardware, printer and material profile are unspecified. No slicer project, G-code or physical print test was supplied. The table describes geometric process needs; inspect actual layer/toolpath previews before printing.

PETG is the project's intended prototype material; PLA can serve for indoor fit trials. Use the printer/material manufacturer's calibrated profile. As a provisional assessment basis, a 0.4 mm nozzle, approximately 0.45 mm extrusion width and 0.2 mm layers are assumed. [Prusa's design guidance](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/modeling-with-3d-printing-in-mind_164135) explains why wall resolution, bridges, orientations and support capability depend on settings. Three/four nominal paths are about 1.35/1.8 mm; this is a geometric screen, not a strength guarantee.

## Fit and hardware acceptance

1. Verify slicer import scale and inspect every layer for missing perimeters, unintended islands, trapped supports, bridge sag and hole closure. Keep the exported orientations. Repair notices are not expected; investigate any slicer repair rather than accepting it silently.
2. Print the coupon with the intended profile; gauge bores and revise CAD allowances. Test one actual motor saddle and wheel/hub before a full vehicle set. Confirm no bolt forces an alignment change.
3. Confirm motor can/gearbox lengths independently, including any front pilot, shaft offset, terminals and encoder. The illustrative 25 mm can + 6 mm gearbox and 8 mm shaft do not identify a purchasable N20.
4. Check tie channels, can contact, motor torque restraint, wiring bend radius, and removal sequence. The motor needs upper-deck and its wheel/hub removal to lift out.
5. Load three M3 nuts into the hub's **inboard** pockets and its radial nut through the top channel. Fit the wheel with its radial access window aligned at 30°. Insert axial bolts from the **outboard wheel face**.
6. **Wheel bolt length matters:** use **M3×14 effective under-head length**, or **M3×16 with 2 mm head-side spacers/washers**. Tips must be flush with the inboard hub face; verify full nut engagement and actual tolerances. Unspaced M3×16 tips collide with the motor saddles, as demonstrated by the hardware control test.
7. Use a **headless M3 grub screw** for the radial lock; a headed screw is not supported by this pocket/window. The nominal probe used a 10 mm screw and Ø3 mm Allen tool; actual tip and shaft flat/engagement remain unverified. Avoid excess tip or bolt protrusion.
8. Prototype minimum axial moving clearance is 0.5 mm between hub/wheel face and saddle nose; runout and printing error can consume it. Hand-spin all wheels through a full turn and revise the interface if contact occurs. No fit certification is possible without the real motor.
9. Measure complete screw stacks before selecting lengths. Long spacer bolts pass base+34 mm spacer+deck, with the front pair also passing the camera foot. Below-base nuts/bolt tips reduce the nominal 5 mm chassis clearance. The modeled 4.949 mm ground clearance excludes that hardware.
10. Check actual pack including connector within 62×32 plan space and available 28.6 mm height allocation; remove the upper deck for access. Verify straps carry retention and screws do not press on cells.
11. Provide measured mounting and connector clearances for controller, camera, three sensor breakouts, both drivers, regulator, switch and wiring. Spare deck space is not a completed electronics mounting design. The installed battery floor covers the central base cable openings; use the deck route and measure the real harness instead of assuming an assembled through-wire path.
12. Perform real assembly, spin, retention and low-load driving checks before relying on strength, traction or clearance. Production acceptance remains pending those results.
